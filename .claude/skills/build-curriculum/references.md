## Free-source preference order

When designing reading lists, prefer in this order:

### 1. Open-access journal articles

- **SSRN** (ssrn.com) — social sciences, often author-uploaded preprints
- **PsyArXiv** (psyarxiv.com) — psychology preprints
- **arXiv** (arxiv.org) — technical, AI, physics
- **ResearchGate** — author uploads (variable quality of access)
- **PubMed Central** — psychology, neuroscience
- **DOAJ** (doaj.org) — directory of open-access journals
- **Google Scholar** — search to find author-hosted PDFs

### 2. Open courseware (OCW) — full lecture series

- **MIT OpenCourseWare** (ocw.mit.edu) — vast collection
- **Yale Open Courses** (oyc.yale.edu)
- **Harvard Online** (online.harvard.edu) — some free
- **Stanford Online** (online.stanford.edu)
- **Carnegie Mellon Open Learning Initiative** (oli.cmu.edu)

### 3. YouTube lectures by canonical authors

Search patterns: `"<author> lecture"`, `"<author> talk"`, `"<author> keynote"`.
Reliable channels: university channels (HarvardBusiness, MIT, Stanford), TED, Long Now Foundation, RSA Animate, Google Talks.

### 4. Audit-mode MOOCs

- **Coursera** — audit-for-free (no certificate)
- **edX** — audit-for-free
- **Class Central** (classcentral.com) — searches across platforms

### 5. Open textbooks

- **OpenStax** (openstax.org) — solid intro-level textbooks
- **BCcampus Open Ed** (open.bccampus.ca)
- **OER Commons** (oercommons.org)
- **MIT Press direct-to-OA titles**
- **Open Textbook Library** (open.umn.edu/opentextbooks)

### 6. Blog / Substack archives of practitioner-academics

- **Ethan Mollick** — *One Useful Thing* (Substack)
- **Adam Grant** — adamgrant.net + Granted Substack
- **Brené Brown** — brenebrown.com (articles + podcast archive)
- **Simon Willison** — simonwillison.net
- **Cal Newport** — calnewport.com
- **Tim Harford, Tyler Cowen (Marginal Revolution), Nadia Asparouhova**
- **Lenny Rachitsky** — leadership/product crossover

### 7. Podcast archives (with transcripts where available)

- **The Knowledge Project** (Shane Parrish) — interviews with practitioners
- **Hidden Brain** (NPR) — psychology applied
- **Ezra Klein Show** — interviews relevant to leadership, AI
- **Dwarkesh Patel** — AI frontier interviews
- **Reboot** (Jerry Colonna) — founder identity
- **Coaching for Leaders** (Dave Stachowiak)

### 8. Library e-book lending

- **Libby** (via local library card) — Overdrive front-end
- **Hoopla** (via local library) — broader catalog, instant access
- **Internet Archive Open Library** (openlibrary.org) — controlled digital lending

### 9. Author personal websites

Many authors host PDFs of papers, book chapters, and unpublished essays. Always check the author's faculty page or personal site before assuming a piece is paywalled.

### 10. Other sources worth checking

- **JSTOR** — many older articles open-access; check
- **HBR.org** — limited free articles per month; many summaries of foundational work
- **McKinsey/BCG/Deloitte insights** — free practitioner-facing pieces (useful for "applied" supplements)
- **edu.gov / WhiteHouse / federal-research repositories** — for AI policy and economic work

## Handling canonical paid texts

When a canonical text (e.g., Kegan's *In Over Our Heads*, Senge's *Fifth Discipline*, Sterman's *Business Dynamics*) is paid-only:

1. **Search for free analogs first:**
   - HBR article by the same author covering same ideas
   - TED/RSA/Long Now talk by the author
   - University lecture series the author has given (often on YouTube)
   - Book excerpts on the author's personal site
   - Edited-volume chapters that summarize the work
   - Published academic reviews (e.g., in *Academy of Management Review*, *Administrative Science Quarterly*) — often available via JSTOR open access or institutional repositories
2. **If the substitute is good:** use it as the primary required reading. Note the substitution explicitly.
3. **If no good substitute exists:** flag the original as `[buy if essential: ~$N]` (estimate cost) and offer it as required. Make clear this is one the user has to decide on.
4. **Never silently drop a canonical work.** Always name it. The user should know what they're trading off.
