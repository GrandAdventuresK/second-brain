# Psychology (contributing discipline)

A **contributing discipline** to the leadership-and-identity major. Provides the empirical floor under any claim about human capacity, change, and behavior — particularly developmental, social, and organizational psychology as they bear on leadership.

See [`/curriculum.md`](../../curriculum.md), [`/through-line.md`](../../through-line.md).

## Lens on the through-line

Leadership-and-identity claims are claims about humans. The discipline that empirically studies humans is psychology. This contributing discipline keeps the major's claims about founders, interior development, and human capacity grounded in actual research rather than in folk theory.

## How this discipline feeds the major

The major draws heavily on developmental psychology (Kegan etc.). It should also know:

- Where developmental claims are empirically supported and where they're not
- What organizational psychology says about behavior in firms
- What social psychology says about identity, role, and group dynamics
- What clinical / health psychology says about burnout, regulation, and recovery (founder-relevant)

Without psychological literacy, the major reads its own field naively — accepting popular framings that don't survive empirical scrutiny.

## Literature map (initial sketch — to develop in `literature.md`)

Scope this discipline to *psychology as it bears on founder/leadership work*, not all of psychology. Schools / lineages:

- **Developmental psychology — adult stages** (Kegan, Loevinger, Cook-Greuter, Torbert) — also held in the major; here studied for empirical foundation
- **Self-determination theory** (Deci, Ryan) — motivation, autonomy, competence, relatedness
- **Organizational psychology** (Edmondson on psychological safety; Grant on motivation at work) — behavior in firms
- **Social psychology of identity** (Tajfel, Turner; current identity research) — role, group, self-concept
- **Affective science / emotion regulation** (Gross) — emotion as data and as load
- **Clinical / health psychology relevant to founders** (Maslach on burnout; recovery and stress research) — sustainability of the founder as instrument
- **Positive psychology — read critically** (Seligman, and the replication-crisis pushback) — what holds up vs. what doesn't
