## Spine and lens

- **Developmental psychology** (Kegan, Cook-Greuter, Torbert, Loevinger) is the **theoretical apparatus**.
- **Founder identity** (Colonna, practitioner-academic founder-coaching tradition) is the **empirical site** where the apparatus is applied.

The dissertation contribution must do both — name a developmental-psychological mechanism *and* show it works in actual founder lives. Neither alone is doctorate-grade for the working question.

The other lineages in `path.md` (adaptive, authentic, vulnerability, contemplative, existential) are **contributing literatures** the spine converses with. Each gets its own course so engagement with the field is defensible, but the original contribution lives in the developmental × founder-identity intersection.

## Prerequisites

You said: *"I don't know what I don't know about this subject."* This section names what you should already have before M1 starts, so you can spot gaps and remediate them in parallel — not discover them mid-Kegan.

Each prereq has: **what it is**, **why it matters here**, **self-check** (how to tell if you have it), **remediation if you don't** (free unless flagged).

### Background-knowledge prereqs (what you should already know)

**P1 — Academic reading literacy.**
*Why here:* Every course assigns peer-reviewed articles. Most founders haven't parsed one in years.
*Self-check:* Open [Leadership Quarterly's free sample issue](https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/the-leadership-quarterly) (no login). Pick one article. Can you state in 3 sentences what the authors claimed, how they tested it, and one limitation?
*Remediation:* Mike Caulfield's SIFT method (free, ~2 hrs) + a couple of YouTube walkthroughs on reading a research paper. ~4 hours total.

**P2 — Basic psychology vocabulary.**
*Why here:* Kegan, Colonna, Brown all assume you know words like *ego, self, identity, unconscious, defense mechanism, transference, projection, attachment*. Without these, the developmental literature reads as mush.
*Self-check:* Can you explain transference and one defense mechanism to a smart non-psychologist in 2 sentences each?
*Remediation:* Yale Open Course — *Intro to Psychology* with Paul Bloom (free, full course on YouTube). Watch units on personality, social psych, and clinical. ~12 hours. OR McWilliams's *Psychoanalytic Diagnosis* — first three chapters often free via Google Books preview.

**P3 — Adult-development literacy.**
*Why here:* Kegan's theory builds *directly* on Piaget. If you don't know what Piaget did with children, Kegan's claim about adults won't land.
*Self-check:* Can you name Piaget's stages and explain what "object permanence" is?
*Remediation:* Crash Course Psychology video on Piaget (10 min, free) + Kegan's own intro chapter of *The Evolving Self* (often free via Google Books preview). ~2 hours.

**P4 — Basic management / organizational-theory vocabulary.**
*Why here:* M1 (Foundations of Leadership Theory) assumes you know what management is, what a strategy is vs. operations, what an org structure is. The leadership canon was written by and for people who already knew Drucker, Mintzberg, Porter.
*Self-check:* In 3 sentences, what's the difference between management and leadership? What's Mintzberg's claim about org structure?
*Remediation:* Drucker's *The Essential Drucker* — available via Libby (library e-lending, free). Plus Mintzberg's HBR articles (free at hbr.org with limited reads, or via library access). ~10 hours skim.

**P5 — Philosophy literacy at intro level.**
*Why here:* M12 (existential/phenomenological) is unreadable without basic philosophy vocabulary. Even earlier courses lean on terms like *ontology, epistemology, phenomenon, hermeneutics, dialectic*.
*Self-check:* Can you explain the difference between ontology (what is) and epistemology (how we know)? Can you say what phenomenology is in 2 sentences?
*Remediation:* Yale Open Course — *Death* with Shelly Kagan (free, full lecture series on YouTube) for existentialism basics. Plus Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entries on phenomenology, ontology, existentialism (free, authoritative). ~8 hours.

**P6 — Research-methods literacy.**
*Why here:* Even though this program is qualitative, you'll read quantitative leadership studies (most of the empirical canon is quantitative). You need to read a regression table without flinching.
*Self-check:* Can you explain what a p-value is, why sample size matters, and what "construct validity" means?
*Remediation:* William Trochim's *Research Methods Knowledge Base* (free at socialresearchmethods.net). Skim the validity, sampling, and measurement sections. ~6 hours.

**P7 — Statistical literacy (minimum floor).**
*Why here:* Same as P6 but specifically for stats. If you've never had stats, P6 won't stick.
*Self-check:* Can you read a table that says "β = 0.34, p < 0.01" and know roughly what it means?
*Remediation:* Khan Academy AP Statistics, first 5 units (free). ~10 hours if you're starting from zero, 2–3 hours of brush-up otherwise.

**P8 — Basic sociology vocabulary.**
*Why here:* Brown, Heifetz, and most organizational scholarship lean on words like *role, norm, institution, power, social construction*. Without these, you'll read past half the claim.
*Self-check:* Can you explain "social construction" and "institutional norm" in plain English?
*Remediation:* Crash Course Sociology playlist (free, ~10 episodes, ~2 hrs total) gets you to floor.

### Skill prereqs (what you should be able to do)

**P9 — Academic writing.**
*Why here:* Quarterly comp papers are real papers, 15–25 pages, citations, defensible claims. When did you last write one?
*Self-check:* Draft a 1-page argument with 3 cited sources in APA. Does it read like an undergrad essay or like scholarship?
*Remediation:* Helen Sword's *Stylish Academic Writing* (Libby). Plus M2 builds writing skill explicitly — so this can be "in progress" at start.

**P10 — Citation and reference management.**
*Why here:* By month 6 you'll have 100+ sources. Random Google docs won't scale.
*Self-check:* Do you have Zotero installed and a working library?
*Remediation:* 1-hour Zotero setup tutorial (free, on YouTube). Set up before M1.

**P11 — Note-taking system for scholarly work.**
*Why here:* Zettelkasten, literature notes, claim-evidence cards — whatever — but you need a *system*. Ad-hoc notes don't survive 18 courses.
*Self-check:* If I asked you "what did Kegan claim about subject-object?" in 18 months, could you find your notes?
*Remediation:* This vault IS the system. Add a `learning/leadership-and-identity/notes/` folder with a consistent format (one note per source, claim-first). Decide format before M3.

### Tool / access prereqs

**P12 — Library access.**
*Why here:* About 30% of canonical sources are most cheaply gotten via library e-lending. Without it, the source-freedom audit's coverage drops noticeably.
*Action:* Get a library card now if you don't have one. Confirm it supports Libby (most do). Bonus: check if your library does interlibrary loan from research libraries — this unlocks more.

**P13 — Academic-database awareness.**
*Why here:* You need to find papers without paying $40 each.
*Setup:* Bookmark Google Scholar, PubMed Central, DOAJ, JSTOR Open. Set up a free ResearchGate account (authors often upload free versions there). ~30 min.

**P14 — PDF + annotation tool.**
*Why here:* You'll annotate hundreds of PDFs.
*Setup:* Zotero has a built-in annotator. Or use whatever you like (GoodReader, Highlights, Skim on Mac). Just pick one.

### What you do NOT need (common founder fears, addressed)

- **You don't need an MBA.** This program doesn't assume corporate finance or accounting depth. P4 is the relevant floor and it's much smaller than an MBA.
- **You don't need to have read the canon already.** That's literally what the program is for. P1–P8 are about being *able to read*, not having read.
- **You don't need to "be smart enough."** Doctoral work is about persistence, method, and willingness to be wrong in public. Not raw IQ.
- **You don't need a research assistant, an academic advisor, or institutional access.** The source-freedom audit and the validation discipline (`validation.md`) are designed for solo work.
- **You don't need to drop GA work.** The whole curriculum architecture (`curriculum.md`) is built around 10–15 hrs/week sustained.

### Self-assessment protocol — run this before M1

Sit down for 2 hours. Answer these in writing. Be honest.

1. **(P1)** Read one paper from Leadership Quarterly's free sample. Summarize the claim, method, and one limitation in 5 sentences.
2. **(P2)** Define ego, self, identity, transference, defense mechanism — each in plain English, one sentence.
3. **(P3)** Name Piaget's stages and explain object permanence.
4. **(P4)** Explain what Drucker meant by "management" and how leadership differs.
5. **(P5)** Define ontology and epistemology. Define phenomenology.
6. **(P6/P7)** Explain what a p-value is. Explain why sample size matters.
7. **(P8)** Define "social construction" and "institutional norm."
8. **(P9/P10)** Write a 1-page draft argument on any topic with 3 sources cited in APA, using Zotero.

**Scoring:**

- **6–8 comfortable:** Start M1 directly. Do remediation on the 0–2 gaps in parallel (1–2 hrs/week alongside coursework).
- **4–5 comfortable:** Do remediation in parallel with M1+M2. Add ~4–6 weeks of light prep load (3–5 hrs/week extra) during the first paired window.
- **Under 4 comfortable:** Run a **"Foundations" pre-program** before M1 — ~80 hours of remediation across 8 weeks. This is not failure; it's the responsible move. Your prior grad work likely covered some of P1–P11; pre-program just fills the gaps that weren't in your discipline.

## Required core sequence — Courses

### Foundations (run first, paired)

**C1 (M1) — Foundations of Leadership Theory.** Survey: trait → behavioral → situational → transformational → adaptive → developmental → authentic → servant. Builds the field map. Required before C3–C12.

**C2 (M2) — Reading the Field: Method, Critique, Synthesis.** How to read leadership research critically. Schools, debates, gaps. Writes-into the schools section of `literature.md`.

### Developmental spine — theoretical apparatus

**C3 (M3) — Kegan & Constructive-Developmental Theory.** Orders of consciousness, subject-object theory, immunity to change. Free sources: Kegan's HBR articles + HBS *Immunity to Change* talks. Flagged paid: *In Over Our Heads*, *Immunity to Change*.

**C4 (M4) — Action Logics & Stage Theory.** Loevinger's ego development, Torbert's action logics, Cook-Greuter's mature stages. Mostly free via author sites and dissertations.

**C5 (M5) — Developmental Measurement: Subject-Object Interview Practicum.** Methods-heavy. Train the SO interview on 3–5 subjects from your network. Bridges to Phase 2 case studies.

### Founder identity — empirical site

**C6 (M6) — The Founder-Coaching Tradition.** Colonna's *Reboot* (flagged paid), Reboot podcast archive, Hsieh, founder-coaching practitioner literature. Live faculty: Colonna.

**C7 (M7) — Founder Identity in the AI-Shaped Era.** Current writing on founder identity through the AI transition. Mollick, Chesky's letters, Lütke writing. Live debate. Cross-listed with `ai-and-emerging-tech`.

### Contributing schools (engagement with the field)

**C8 (M8) — Adaptive Leadership.** Heifetz / Linsky. Leadership-as-work distinct from authority. Free: HBR articles + Harvard Kennedy lectures. Flagged: *Practice of Adaptive Leadership*.

**C9 (M9) — Authentic Leadership.** George, Avolio — and the critiques (Alvesson, Iszatt-White). Engage the empirical contestation, don't take the school at face value.

**C10 (M10) — Vulnerability & Courage.** Brown's research base, Whyte's poetic register. Relational substrate of leadership.

**C11 (M11) — Contemplative Traditions in Leadership.** Presence, equanimity, non-attachment lineages applied. Eastern and Western mystical sources. Watch for spiritual-appropriation pitfalls.

**C12 (M12) — Existential & Phenomenological Foundations.** Heidegger *Being and Time*, Merleau-Ponty, applied to leadership ontology. Most demanding course. Pair with Dreyfus's free Berkeley lecture series on Heidegger.

## Methods sequence — Courses

Methods are *trained* on real questions, not just read about. Several are integrated inside school courses (SO interview in C5; longitudinal self-observation in C7; coaching-as-inquiry in C6). The methods-specific intensives:

**C13 (ME1) — Case Study Method.** Eisenhardt's process, Yin's design, comparative case logic. Trained on a pilot case from your network.

**C14 (ME2) — Phenomenological Interview.** Seidman's three-interview structure, Vagle's post-intentional phenomenology. Trained on 2–3 pilot interviews.

**C15 (ME3) — Coaching-as-Inquiry.** Coaching practice as research practice. Per-session prediction → observation → revision loop.

## Specialization courses (pick 2–3, taken later)

Selected as the dissertation question narrows. Candidates:

- **C16a (S1) — AI-Shaped Organizational Forms** (cross-listed with `ai-and-emerging-tech`)
- **C16b (S2) — Founder Mental Health & Interior Distress** (burnout, identity collapse)
- **C16c (S3) — Board & Governance for Founder-Led Firms**
- **C16d (S4) — Contemplative-Secular Dialogue**

## Capstone — Prospectus (Phase 1 terminal)

**C18 (T1) — Prospectus Development.** Single intensive course, longer than 6 weeks. Defends the dissertation question, its location in the literature, the proposed method, the case for the work. Written-defense format per `curriculum.md` (no committee; public posting + critic review).

Phase 2 (cases, framework, GA interventions) and Phase 3 (dissertation) are defined in `/curriculum.md`. This program.md scopes Phase 1 only.

## Comprehensive examination

In place of an oral comp exam, this program's comprehensive demonstration is **the quarterly comp paper sequence**:

- Every ~3 months in Phase 1 — one comp paper.
- Each paper makes at least one falsifiable claim, names the falsification condition, is shown to 2–3 named critics before publication, and tracks reception (per `validation.md`).
- The accumulated comp papers function as the doctoral comp portfolio.

## Semester-by-semester sketch (paired sequential)

A "semester" = two consecutive paired-course windows (~12 weeks total). Locked when each `semesters/semester-N.md` is generated.

Six semesters at the paired load = ~72 weeks of paired work + prospectus intensive. Matches `curriculum.md`'s 18–24 month Phase 1 estimate. Add 4–8 weeks for prereq remediation if the self-assessment surfaces gaps.
