# AI & Emerging Tech (contributing discipline)

A **contributing discipline** to the leadership-and-identity major. Provides the substrate understanding — what AI can and cannot do, where it's going, how it deploys — without which any leadership argument for this era is timeless and ungrounded.

See [`/curriculum.md`](../../curriculum.md), [`/through-line.md`](../../through-line.md).

## Lens on the through-line

The **know** layer. A working founder's understanding of the actual AI substrate — capabilities, limits, economics, deployment patterns, second-order effects. Enough to make real bets. Not so much that you confuse yourself with a research engineer.

## How this discipline feeds the major

The dissertation is about founders leading well *through this transition*. The transition is AI. Without a current, accurate understanding of what AI actually does, the leadership argument floats. This discipline keeps the argument grounded in the actual substrate, not in popular accounts of it.

## Literature map (initial sketch — to develop in `literature.md`)

- **Applied / managerial** (Mollick, McKinsey, HBR coverage) — practitioner-facing accounts
- **Technical foundations** (Vaswani et al. *Attention Is All You Need*; scaling-law papers; current frontier papers) — the substrate itself
- **Alignment / safety** (Russell *Human Compatible*; Christian *The Alignment Problem*; Anthropic and DeepMind publications) — what could go wrong
- **Critique** (Bender, Gebru, Crawford) — pushback on hype and harm
- **Economic / societal** (Acemoglu, Brynjolfsson) — labor markets, productivity, distribution
- **Frontier reporting** (Karpathy talks, Simon Willison's blog, Import AI newsletter, Dwarkesh Patel interviews) — the living substrate
