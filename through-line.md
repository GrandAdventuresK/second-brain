# Through-line

The unifying question this whole vault is in service of. Expected to evolve.

This file sits separately from [`curriculum.md`](curriculum.md) on purpose — so the question can move without forcing the strategic architecture to move with it. Treat the current articulation as a hypothesis to operate against, not as fixed truth.

## Current articulation

> **v0 — *hypothesis, 2026-05-23*:**
>
> *How do humans and AI build excellent organizations together — and what does a founder need to know, be, and do to lead them?*

### Why this question, why now

It triangulates the three current learning paths:

- *Leadership & Identity* → the **be** layer
- *AI & Emerging Tech* → the **know** layer
- *Systems Thinking* → the **do** layer

It grounds the abstraction in a concrete role (founder) and a concrete context (Grand Adventures). It admits that this is a transitional era — that the answer requires both technical fluency and human depth, and that neither alone is enough.

### What I'm holding loosely

- **The phrasing.** "Humans and AI" might be the wrong frame. "People, AI, and capital" might cut closer. "Founder-led firms in an AI-shaped era" might cut closer still.
- **The scope.** "Organizations" is broad. The real question might be specifically about founder-led organizations, or specifically about Grand Adventures, or specifically about a class of firms I haven't named yet.
- **The pronoun.** The question might shift from third-person (*how do founders*) to first-person (*how do I*) as the work matures and the abstraction stops being useful.

## When to revisit

- After every path completes Phase 1 — a teachable synthesis usually exposes whether the question still fits.
- After every comp paper. Public writing forces articulation, which exposes the question's edges.
- When a `notes/` cluster surfaces that doesn't fit the current framing.
- At minimum: once per quarter.

## How to revise

Don't overwrite. Append a new version below with the date and what pushed it to shift. The history is part of the work — knowing *how* the question moved tells you what you actually learned.
